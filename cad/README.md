## tolerance_coupon_v1.stl — PRD M1.3

**Why this exists:** nominal Lego geometry (pin hole Ø4.8 mm, beam pitch
8.0 mm) does not print at nominal on an FDM machine — holes come out
undersize, and by an amount specific to *your* printer, filament, nozzle and
slicer settings. Community starting points are ~5.1 mm for a pin fit and
5.3–5.6 mm for a free-rotating axle bore, but those are **unverified on this
printer**, which is the whole point of this part. Every chassis dimension in
M1.5–M1.7 depends on the numbers you get here, so this is the first thing to
print and it gates the rest of M1.

Generated by [`scripts/gen_tolerance_coupon.py`](../scripts/gen_tolerance_coupon.py) —
edit the constants there and re-run rather than editing the STL.

```bash
python scripts/gen_tolerance_coupon.py
```

The generator self-validates and refuses to claim success quietly: it checks
the mesh is closed and consistently wound (every directed edge appears once,
its reverse once) and that the signed volume matches the analytic expectation.
Current output: `manifold PASS`, `volume PASS` at 6.69e-16 relative error,
18,048 triangles, 104 × 56 × 8 mm.

### What's on the plate

| Feature | Layout | Diameters | How to identify |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitch row** | 5 holes at exactly **8.00 mm** centres | all 5.10 mm | the tightly-spaced row, no markers |
| **Pin row** | 6 holes at 16 mm centres | 4.80 → 5.30 in 0.1 steps | **one** 3 mm marker hole at the left end |
| **Axle row** | 6 holes at 16 mm centres | 5.20 → 5.70 in 0.1 steps | **two** 3 mm marker holes, one at each end |

Sweeps ascend left-to-right from the marker end. The plate is 8 mm thick on
purpose — that matches a real Lego beam, and hole shrinkage is depth-dependent,
so a thin coupon would give you optimistic numbers.

### Printing

**Print it with the exact settings you intend to use for the chassis.** If you
later change layer height, nozzle, or filament brand, these numbers stop being
valid and the coupon has to be re-run. Record the settings you used — they go
in the record entry alongside the measurements.

- No supports needed; all holes are vertical through-holes.
- Suggested starting point: 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, ≥20% infill, 0.4 mm nozzle.
- **Print it twice — once in PLA, once in PETG.** The research calls for PETG
  on rotating bores (PLA galls against Lego axles) and PLA is fine for static
  holes, so you need both sets of numbers. ~15 min each.

### Testing

You need real Lego parts and calipers.

- **Pin row — pass = a Lego pin pushes in with firm thumb pressure and HOLDS.**
  Too tight if you need pliers or the plate creaks; too loose if it falls out
  or wobbles. Record the smallest diameter that seats without excessive force.
- **Axle row — pass = a Lego axle spins FREELY with no perceptible radial
  slop.** Record the smallest diameter that spins freely, since larger means
  more slop.
- **Pitch row — insert two pins joined by a real Lego beam (or a 2-pin
  connector) across holes 1 and 2, then across 1 and 5.** If the beam seats on
  adjacent holes but binds across all five, your printer has a dimensional
  scaling error — measure the actual centre-to-centre distance across the 5
  holes with calipers (nominal 32.00 mm) and note the ratio. That scaling
  factor applies to every chassis part.
- Also caliper each hole's **actual printed diameter** so you know the offset
  between commanded and achieved, not just which hole happens to work.

### When you're done

1. Put the filled table + print settings into a record entry (they are
   portfolio evidence — this is a real measured experiment, not a guess).
2. Write the two chosen offsets into `.claude/codebase-memory/gotchas.md`,
   replacing the unverified community figures currently there.
3. M1.5 onward can then model against real numbers.

**If the sweep brackets nothing** — every pin hole too tight, or every axle
bore too loose — edit the ranges at the top of the generator and re-run rather
than guessing beyond the ends of the sweep.

## servo_lego_coupler_v1.stl — PRD task 8c(ii)

**Why this exists:** the servo-to-pinion coupling is the highest-torque joint
on the car (Appendix BV). The chosen part is the Adafruit #4252 ($0.75), but
Adafruit is a sole-source vendor for it and charges ~$10 shipping for that one
item (Evan, 2026-09-03). This is a printed stopgap to try first — **not a
proven replacement.**

Generated by [`scripts/gen_servo_lego_coupler.py`](../scripts/gen_servo_lego_coupler.py) —
edit the constants there and re-run rather than editing the STL.

```bash
python scripts/gen_servo_lego_coupler.py
```

Self-validates the same way the coupon does (closed + consistently wound mesh,
signed volume against an analytic expectation) and refuses to write on failure.
Current output: `manifold PASS`, `volume PASS` at 1.05e-14 relative error,
1,152 triangles, Ø10 × 16 mm, 1015.12 mm³.

### The design, and the one thing it deliberately does not do

| end | feature | why |
|---|---|---|
| servo (z 0–8) | Ø4.65 mm plain round bore | 0.20 mm under the 4.85 mm 20T spline; the metal spline cuts its own seat |
| middle (z 8–10) | Ø2.2 mm axial hole | screwdriver access to the servo's OWN retaining screw — zero new parts, stops the coupler walking off the shaft |
| Lego (z 10–16) | 5.00 × 2.00 mm cross socket, 6 mm deep | receives a **real** Lego axle |

**It prints no cross-axle profile.** Appendix BV measured a printed male cross
profile at SF 0.57–0.96 (thin cruciform fins shearing along layer lines) and
`steering.md` permits socket-style printed adapters only. This obeys that: the
printed part is a thick-walled socket (2.50 mm wall at the socket, 2.68 mm at
the bore); the cross profile is the real Lego axle's.

**The spline is not modelled, on purpose.** 20 teeth on a Ø4.85 mm circle is a
~0.76 mm tooth pitch — under what a 0.4 mm nozzle resolves. A modelled spline
would print as a blurred round hole while claiming fidelity it does not have.

### ⚠️ What is NOT known

**No safety factor is claimed, and none can be computed** without a torque
test. The bore-to-spline grip is friction plus spline bite and has never been
loaded. What protects this joint is the existing rule that the servo never
reaches stall (`SERVO_US_SPAN` limiting, task 8c). If it slips, the upgrade is
a transverse M2 grub screw into the bore — **not** the MG996R, which makes the
coupling problem ~5× worse (BOM row 7).

### Four numbers to caliper, then re-run

| constant | current | status |
|---|---|---|
| `SPLINE_DIA` | 4.85 | datasheet range 4.8–4.9; measure the real servo |
| `AXLE_ARM` | 1.80 | Lego cross-axle arm thickness — community value, **unverified**. The 4.80 mm across-the-cross span IS confirmed |
| `SOCKET_CLEAR` | 0.20 | provisional; the right source is the M1.3 coupon's axle row, unprinted |
| `BORE_INTERF` | 0.20 | press interference — a guess; the first print settles it |

Print it in **PETG**, not PLA: this is a loaded joint and PLA is the material
Appendix BV's failing numbers were computed against.
